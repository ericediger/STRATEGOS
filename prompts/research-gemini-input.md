## Research Instructions

Produce an **exhaustive, comprehensive research report** on the assignment above. This report will be consumed by a downstream analysis agent that synthesizes raw research into strategic deliverables. Your job is to provide the richest, most detailed raw material possible.

### Depth Expectations

- **Comprehensive reports of 10,000–25,000 words are expected** for complex competitive research topics. Do not aim for brevity. Length should reflect the depth of available information.
- For every entity in scope, cover: company overview, financial signals, product landscape, pricing and packaging, go-to-market strategy, competitive positioning, strategic signals (hiring, partnerships, M&A), weaknesses and risks, recent changes (last 90 days), and forward-looking assessment.
- Include **specific evidence throughout**: revenue figures, funding amounts, dates, names, pricing tiers, customer counts, headcount numbers, direct quotes from sources, and URLs.
- When you find something interesting, **explore it in depth**. When sources conflict, present both sides with evidence.

### What NOT to Do

- **Do not summarize.** Do not write an executive brief. Do not compress findings.
- **Do not limit yourself to bullet points.** Write with the depth and specificity of a professional analyst report.
- If you have evidence on a topic, include all of it — not a condensed version.
- The single most common failure mode is producing a thin, high-level summary instead of deep research. Resist this.

### Source Standards

- Cite every factual claim inline: `[Source Name — Date]`
- Prefer primary sources (SEC filings, pricing pages, changelogs, job postings, engineering blogs) over secondary coverage
- Seek evidence from multiple independent sources for critical claims
- Investigate negative signals (layoffs, complaints, churn, outages, lawsuits) — not just positive narrative
- When a claim relies on a single source, note it as `[Single Source]`

### Output Format

Structure your report by entity (primary target first, then competitors in order of strategic importance). Use headers, subheaders, and tables where they add clarity. Include direct quotes from sources when illuminating. Write in flowing paragraphs with full context — not compressed bullet lists.

**Produce your research now. Be thorough. Be exhaustive. Leave nothing out.**
